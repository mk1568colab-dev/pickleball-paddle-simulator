One Round, Four Steps

  1. Read the market. Review the demand outlook range, market summary, segment mix, competitor prices, and previous results. The midpoint is a planning scenario, not guaranteed demand or your firm's sales.
  2. Coordinate and submit. Agree on product forecasts and prices, then align materials, production, capacity, development, and cash. Save decisions before submissions close.
  3. Instructor runs the round. Actual demand is fixed when submissions first open and stays hidden during decisions, including if submissions are closed and reopened.
  4. Review and adjust. Actual market demand is disclosed after results. Compare forecasts, sales, service, inventory, profit, cash, and debt.

Your Starting Firm

Teams begin under equal conditions with four product slots (A-D). Premium Control (premium, $189) and Starter Play (beginner, $99) are active. Slots C-D are vacant and require development and launch. Starting cash is $50,000, installed capacity is 300 units, and raw-material inventory is 300 units.

Four Decision Checks

  • Market and products: segment, price, forecast, product status, quality spending, finished-goods inventory target, and retirement.
  • Supply and operations: supplier mix, material orders, expected arrivals, expediting, production, overtime, capacity expansion, and backlog tolerance.
  • Future products: investment, technology, testing intensity, planned launch round, launch request, and replacement slot.
  • Cash and submission: cash needs, borrowing, warnings, and saved decisions. A profitable plan can still create a cash shortage.

Launch and Replacement

Launch requires full project funding, sufficient readiness, an eligible round, and an explicit launch request. At most one product can launch per team per round. A vacant slot adds a product; an occupied slot replaces its product. Review the launch preview before submitting.

Investment and testing effort accumulate separately. Testing coverage is capped at 100%. Two rounds at intensity 0.5 cost $500 each; one round at intensity 1.0 costs $2,000. Both achieve the same coverage, but spreading testing takes longer. Testing expense reduces cash and profit and does not count toward the funding gate. Set intensity to zero once testing coverage is complete to avoid unnecessary spending.

Debrief Beyond Profit

Consider profitability alongside service, forecast error, supply reliability, inventory, capacity use, cash stability, and debt. Identify which decisions fit the market and which created avoidable risk. Follow your instructor's grading rubric.
